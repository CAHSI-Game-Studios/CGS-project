# DialogueSystem

## Purpose

The **DialogueSystem** manages conversations between the player and characters throughout *The Arcade*.

---

## Starting Dialogue

A dialogue interaction begins when the player attempts to interact with a character that supports conversation.

Before starting the conversation, the DialogueSystem should determine whether:

- The character can currently be spoken to.
- The character has dialogue available.
- Any conditions required for the conversation have been satisfied.
- A different conversation should be used because of the player's previous actions or discoveries.

Once dialogue begins:

- Normal player movement or unrelated interactions should be temporarily disabled.
- The speaking character should be identified.
- The appropriate starting dialogue entry should be selected.
- The dialogue interface should become active.

---

## Character Dialogue

Each character that can participate in conversations should provide the DialogueSystem with access to their dialogue content.

A character's dialogue may contain:

- Dialogue spoken by the character.
- Dialogue spoken by the player.
- Player response options.
- Connections to other dialogue entries.
- Conditions that determine whether dialogue or choices are available.
- Information or game state changes caused by the conversation.
- One or more possible conversation endings.

Characters are responsible for their own personality, wording, story content, and conversation structure.

The DialogueSystem is responsible for interpreting that structure and presenting the conversation consistently.

---

## Conversation Structure

A conversation is made up of individual **dialogue entries**.

Each dialogue entry represents one step in a conversation and may contain:

- A speaker.
- Dialogue text.
- A next dialogue entry.
- Multiple possible next entries.
- Optional player choices.
- Optional conditions.
- Optional effects caused by reaching or selecting that entry.

A simple conversation may follow a direct sequence:

`Dialogue A → Dialogue B → Dialogue C → End`

More complex conversations may branch:

```text
Character Dialogue
        |
        v
Player Choice
   /         \
Choice A     Choice B
   |            |
   v            v
Dialogue A   Dialogue B
```

Different branches may reconnect later or lead to completely different conversation endings.

---

## Player Dialogue Choices

Some dialogue entries may allow the player to choose between multiple responses.

When choices are available:

- The conversation should pause until the player selects an option.
- Only currently available choices should be presented.
- The selected choice determines which dialogue entry is visited next.
- A choice may also update information about the player or game world.

Choices may affect:

- The current conversation.
- Future conversations with the same character.
- Conversations with other characters.
- Information available elsewhere in the game.

Characters are not required to provide player choices. Linear conversations should also be supported.

---

## Dialogue Conditions

Dialogue entries and player choices may have conditions that determine whether they are available.

Examples include:

- The player has spoken to a specific character before.
- The player discovered a particular piece of information.
- The player completed an event or activity.
- The player selected a certain response in an earlier conversation.
- The character is currently in a particular state.
- A global game condition has changed.

Conditions may determine:

- Which conversation begins.
- Which dialogue entry appears.
- Which player choices are visible.
- Which dialogue branch becomes available.
- Whether a character can currently be spoken to.

The DialogueSystem should evaluate these conditions but should not be responsible for owning all of the information used by them.

---

## Previously Discovered Information

Information learned elsewhere in *The Arcade* may influence conversations.

For example, the player may discover information by:

- Talking to another character.
- Examining an object.
- Completing a minigame.
- Visiting a location.
- Triggering a story event.

The DialogueSystem should be able to check this information when determining which dialogue or choices are available.

The DialogueSystem should **read** relevant game state from the system responsible for tracking that information rather than maintaining a separate duplicate version of the same state.

Dialogue may also report new discoveries back to the appropriate game system when something important is learned during a conversation.

---

## Ending Dialogue

A conversation ends when:

- A dialogue entry explicitly ends the conversation.
- The player selects an option that ends the conversation.
- The conversation reaches the end of its current branch.

When dialogue ends, the DialogueSystem should:

- Close the dialogue interface.
- Clear the active conversation and character.
- Apply any final conversation effects.
- Return control to normal gameplay.
- Allow the player to move and interact again.

A conversation may end permanently or may allow the player to speak with the character again later.

---

## Information Required From a Character

A character that supports dialogue should provide enough information for the DialogueSystem to determine:

- Which character is speaking.
- What dialogue belongs to that character.
- Which dialogue entry should begin the conversation.
- Whether an alternate starting conversation should be used.
- What dialogue entries and choices are available.
- Where each dialogue entry leads.
- When the conversation should end.

Character-specific systems may also provide information such as the character's current state when that state affects dialogue.

---

## Collaborator-Created Dialogue

Collaborators should be able to create dialogue for their own characters without changing the core DialogueSystem.

Conceptually, each character's dialogue should describe:

1. **What is said**
2. **Who is speaking**
3. **What happens next**
4. **Which choices the player can make, if any**
5. **What conditions must be satisfied**
6. **What changes when a dialogue entry or choice occurs**
7. **When the conversation ends**

The DialogueSystem should remain generic enough that characters can have very different conversation styles.

For example, one character may have a completely linear conversation while another may have several branching conversations influenced by information discovered throughout the game.

---

## Domain Boundaries

### DialogueSystem is responsible for

- Starting dialogue interactions.
- Keeping track of the active conversation.
- Progressing between dialogue entries.
- Presenting available player choices.
- Evaluating dialogue conditions using information provided by other systems.
- Triggering dialogue-related effects.
- Ending conversations.
- Returning control to normal gameplay.

### Character Domain is responsible for

- Character identity.
- Character personality.
- Character-specific dialogue content.
- Character-specific conversation branches.
- Character states that may influence dialogue.

### Player / Interaction Domain is responsible for

- Detecting when the player attempts to interact with a character.
- Determining whether the player is within interaction range.
- Passing the interaction request to the DialogueSystem.
- Handling normal player controls outside of dialogue.

### Game State / Information Domain is responsible for

- Tracking important discoveries.
- Tracking completed events or progression.
- Tracking persistent decisions made by the player.
- Providing this information when dialogue conditions need to be evaluated.

### UI Domain is responsible for

- Displaying character dialogue.
- Displaying the current speaker.
- Displaying player response options.
- Communicating the player's selected option back to the DialogueSystem.

The DialogueSystem determines **what should happen in the conversation**, while the UI determines **how that conversation is displayed to the player**.
